Aboveground tank inspections

Aboveground storage tanks should be inspected on a regular basis to help identify corrosion, leaks, damage, coating failure, and other conditions that could affect the tank’s ability to remain in service.

For facilities subject to SPCC requirements, EPA requires bulk storage containers to be inspected and tested for integrity in accordance with an appropriate industry standard. STI SP001 is commonly used for shop-fabricated aboveground storage tanks.

What is integrity testing?

Integrity testing evaluates the condition of an aboveground storage tank and helps determine whether the tank is suitable for continued service.

Depending on the tank, testing may include:

  • Visual inspection of the tank shell, supports, welds, fittings, and foundation
  • Inspection for corrosion, leaks, deformation, or coating deterioration
  • Ultrasonic thickness testing
  • Review of secondary containment and leak detection
  • Formal external inspection
  • Formal internal inspection when required
  • Leak testing when required

Not every tank requires the same type of inspection or the same inspection interval. The inspection schedule depends on factors such as the tank’s size, construction, secondary containment, and release detection.

What is ultrasonic thickness testing?

Ultrasonic thickness testing, or UT testing, is a nondestructive testing method used to measure the remaining thickness of the tank’s steel.

An ultrasonic gauge sends sound waves through the steel and measures the material thickness without cutting into or damaging the tank.

These measurements can help identify areas of metal loss and corrosion and provide documentation of the tank’s condition at the time of inspection.

Tank locations, capacities, contents, approximate age, nameplate photos, and previous inspection records if available.

STI SP001 tank categories

STI SP001 uses tank categories to help determine the appropriate inspection schedule.

Category 1

Tank has spill control and Continuous Release Detection Method (CRDM).

Category 2

Tank has spill control but does not have CRDM.

Category 3

Tank does not have spill control.

The tank category, along with its capacity, determines which inspections and tests are required and how often they are performed.

Understanding the inspection schedule

PPeriodic Inspection
Routine inspection performed by the owner or owner’s inspector.
EFormal External Inspection
A documented external inspection performed by a qualified/certified tank inspector.
IFormal Internal Inspection
A documented inspection of the interior and exterior of the tank performed by a qualified/certified tank inspector.
LLeak Test
A test used to determine whether the tank is liquid-tight.

Numbers in parentheses indicate the maximum inspection interval in years.
For example, E(5) means a Formal External Inspection at a maximum interval of five years.

When you may need a tank inspection

  • Your SPCC Plan identifies an upcoming integrity inspection.
  • Your tank has reached its STI SP001 inspection interval.
  • You do not have previous inspection or thickness records.
  • You have noticed corrosion, coating failure, leakage, or physical damage.
  • A tank has been repaired, modified, or relocated.
  • You need baseline thickness measurements for an existing tank.
  • You need documentation showing the condition of your tank.
